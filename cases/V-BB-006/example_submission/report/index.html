Vision Transformers with a Convolutional Stem and Average Pooling for Small Data

ABench reference submissionStandard reference for task statement standard v2.1
Abstract

This submission keeps the anchor's Vision Transformer (al., 2021) at the ViT-Ti and ViT-S sizes and makes two changes known to help when data is scarce: the 16 × 16 patchify layer is replaced by a stem of four stride-2 3 × 3 convolutions (Xiao et al., 2021), and the class token and learned position embeddings are replaced by global average pooling and fixed 2-D sine-cosine embeddings (Beyer et al., 2022). The large variant drops one of its twelve blocks to stay within its budget. It has not been run yet: it needs PyTorch and a GPU, which were not available where the case was built.

1Introduction

Vision transformers trained from scratch on ten thousand images fall well behind convolutional networks, because they must learn locality and translation equivariance from data. With the recipe fixed, the backbone has to supply those priors without exceeding its compute budget.

Changes relative to the anchor. Each item states what this submission does differently and where it is in the code.

  1. A stem of four stride-2 3 × 3 convolutions and a 1 × 1 convolution replaces the 16 × 16 patchify layer. — model.py: ConvStemViT.__init__
  2. Global average pooling and fixed 2-D sine-cosine position embeddings replace the class token and learned position embeddings. — model.py: sincos_2d, ConvStemViT.forward
  3. The large variant has 11 instead of 12 blocks to stay within its compute budget. — model.py: build

2Related Work

The anchor showed that a plain transformer over image patches matches convolutional networks with large-scale pre-training (al., 2021); DeiT trained it on ImageNet alone with strong augmentation and distillation (Touvron et al., 2021). A convolutional stem improves optimisation stability and accuracy at equal compute (Xiao et al., 2021); simple baselines with average pooling and fixed position embeddings train better in short schedules (Beyer et al., 2022); small-data studies add locality to attention or shift tokens (Lee et al., 2021).

3Method

The stem has four 3 × 3 convolutions with stride 2 and widths d/8, d/4, d/2 and d, each with batch normalisation and ReLU, followed by a 1 × 1 convolution, giving a 10 × 10 grid of tokens at 160 pixels. Fixed sine-cosine embeddings of the two grid coordinates are added. The blocks are the baseline's pre-norm blocks (12 for the small variant with width 192, 11 for the large one with width 384); the head reads the mean of the final tokens.

4Design Rationale

Overlapping small convolutions give the early representation the locality that a single linear patch projection lacks, at a cost of about 0.1 GFLOPs; average pooling uses every token for the classification and removes a parameter that has to be learned from few images; fixed position embeddings remove another.

5Scope and Limitations

The changes do not touch the attention itself; dropping a block in the large variant trades depth for the stem; and the reference has not been run yet.
