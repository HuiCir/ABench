A RandAugment-Style Policy with Cutout and a Clean Final Phase

ABench reference submissionStandard reference for task statement standard v2.1
Abstract

This submission gives a reference augmentation policy for the fixed 40-epoch CIFAR-10 recipe. It replaces the anchor's learned sub-policies (Cubuk et al., 2019) by RandAugment's two hyperparameters (Cubuk et al., 2020): after the standard flip and translation, each image receives two operations drawn uniformly from nine at a shared magnitude, then an 8 × 8 cutout (DeVries & Taylor, 2017). The last 10% of the epochs use flip and translation only. No search was run for the reference; the magnitude is the common default for CIFAR-10, which leaves the search procedure itself to entries.

1Introduction

Augmentation policies trade the diversity of training images against their fidelity to the test distribution, and the best trade-off depends on the network and the length of training. With a short recipe, strong policies can hurt; with a longer one, they usually help.

Changes relative to the anchor. Each item states what this submission does differently and where it is in the code.

  1. The anchor's searched sub-policies are replaced by RandAugment's uniform choice of two operations at one magnitude. — policy.py: augment
  2. Cutout is applied after the operations, and the last 10% of the epochs use only flip and translation. — policy.py: augment
  3. Each drawn operation runs only on the images that drew it, on the whole training set at once. — policy.py: augment (masks)

2Related Work

AutoAugment searched sub-policies of two operations with probabilities and magnitudes using reinforcement learning on a reduced proxy task (Cubuk et al., 2019); Fast AutoAugment matched augmented and clean data distributions with density matching instead of training (Lim et al., 2019). RandAugment showed that sampling operations uniformly at a single magnitude performs as well with two hyperparameters (Cubuk et al., 2020), and TrivialAugment that one random operation at a random strength needs none (Müller & Hutter, 2021). Cutout removes random square regions (DeVries & Taylor, 2017).

These policies were tuned for training runs of hundreds of epochs; which search procedure finds the best policy for a 40-epoch recipe within three GPU-hours is the open question.

3Method

3.1Policy

Every image is flipped with probability 0.5 and translated by up to four pixels. For two rounds, each image draws one of nine operations — rotation up to 9°, horizontal or vertical shear up to 0.09, brightness, contrast, saturation and sharpness changes up to 27%, posterisation to six bits or solarisation above 0.7 — and the drawn operation is applied to those images only. An 8 × 8 cutout follows. From epoch 36 of 40 only flip and translation remain.

3.2Complexity

Per epoch, each image passes through four transformations on the GPU; each operation runs only on its subset, so the cost is about four whole-set passes, small next to an epoch of training.

4Design Rationale

A single magnitude avoids a search over per-operation strengths that a 40-epoch recipe cannot amortise, and the clean final phase lets the network adapt to unaugmented images before testing.

5Scope and Limitations

The magnitude and the clean-phase length were not searched, the operation list is fixed, and nothing adapts the policy to the network's progress during training.
