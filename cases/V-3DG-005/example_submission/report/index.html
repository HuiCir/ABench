Random Backgrounds and an Anisotropy Penalty for Sparse-View Gaussian Splatting

ABench reference submissionStandard reference for task statement standard v2.1
Abstract

This submission is plain 3D Gaussian splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023) from a random initialisation with two changes for sparse views: a random background colour at every training step, and a penalty on Gaussians whose largest scale exceeds ten times their smallest, after the anisotropy regulariser of PhysGaussian (Xie et al., 2024). Both target artefacts that fit the 24 training views but fail elsewhere; neither adds a prior from outside the scene.

1Introduction

With 24 views of an unbounded scene, Gaussian splatting overfits: semi-transparent Gaussians imitate the background and needle-shaped ones explain single views. Sparse-view methods such as FSGS add densification rules and depth priors (Zhu et al., 2024); this submission tests two cheap regularisers that need no prior.

Changes relative to the anchor. Each item states what this submission does differently and where it is in the code.

  1. A random background colour at every step instead of a fixed one. — method.py: fit; src/gs3d.py: fit
  2. A penalty on Gaussian anisotropy above a ratio of 10. — method.py: _anisotropy

2Related Work

3D Gaussian splatting optimises anisotropic Gaussians with a differentiable rasteriser (Kerbl et al., 2023); FSGS grows Gaussians by proximity and uses monocular depth for sparse views (Zhu et al., 2024); PhysGaussian limits the ratio of a Gaussian's scales (Xie et al., 2024); Mip-NeRF 360 introduced the scenes (Barron et al., 2022).

3Method

The baseline's 7,000-step schedule, losses and densification are kept. Each step renders against a background colour drawn uniformly at random; the loss adds 0.01 times the mean of max(0, s_max / s_min - 10) over Gaussians.

4Design Rationale

A random background makes any Gaussian that stands in for empty space pay a loss on most steps; bounding anisotropy keeps Gaussians from being thin enough to be invisible from all but one viewpoint.

5Scope and Limitations

Initialisation is still random in a sphere, and unseen regions get no geometric regularisation. Nothing here has been run in this build.
