Sparse Identification with Smoothed Derivatives, Scale-Free Thresholds and Cross-Validated Supports

ABench reference submissionStandard reference for task statement standard v2.1
Abstract

This submission keeps the sparse-regression formulation of SINDy (Brunton et al., 2016) and changes the three places where the anchor's sequentially thresholded least squares (STLSQ) is fragile on unseen systems: derivatives are estimated with Savitzky-Golay smoothing (Savitzky & Golay, 1964) whose window follows a noise estimate, thresholds act on a column-normalised term library so that they do not depend on variable scales, and each equation's support is chosen along a path of thresholds by leave-one-trajectory-out error, keeping the sparsest support within 10% of the best. On three development sets of 20 systems drawn by the case's generator with public seeds, it recovers the exact structure of 65%, 35% and 50% of the systems against 30%, 35% and 50% for the anchor's STLSQ, with a lower extrapolation error on all three; these are calibration checks, not results of the case.

1Introduction

Equation discovery asks for the terms of an unknown vector field, not only for a good fit. SINDy casts it as sparse regression of estimated derivatives on a library of candidate terms (Brunton et al., 2016); the anchor's STLSQ alternates least squares with hard thresholding at a fixed level. On systems it has not been tuned for, three choices decide whether the structure comes out right: how derivatives are estimated from noisy samples, how the threshold relates to coefficient scales, and how the final support is selected.

Changes relative to the anchor. Each item states what this submission does differently and where it is in the code.

  1. Derivatives come from Savitzky-Golay smoothing with a window chosen from a noise estimate, instead of finite differences of the raw samples. — discover.py: _noise_level, _derivative
  2. Thresholding acts on a library whose columns are scaled to unit RMS, so one threshold path serves every variable scale. — discover.py: discover
  3. Each equation's support is chosen along a path of 30 thresholds by leave-one-trajectory-out error (the sparsest within 10% of the best) instead of one fixed threshold. — discover.py: discover, TOL

2Related Work

The anchor introduced the library regression and STLSQ (Brunton et al., 2016); PDE-FIND extended it to partial differential equations with ridge-regularised thresholding (Rudy et al., 2017). Model selection over candidate supports with information criteria was studied by Mangan et al. (Mangan et al., 2017). Weak formulations replace pointwise derivatives by integrals against test functions and are markedly more robust to noise (Messenger & Bortz, 2021); ensembles over bootstrapped data give inclusion probabilities for each term (Fasel et al., 2022); relaxed sparse optimisation unifies several thresholding schemes (Champion et al., 2020).

The reference uses only pointwise derivatives and a deterministic support path, so weak forms and ensembles remain open directions for entries.

3Method

3.1Derivatives

For each trajectory, the relative size of second differences estimates the noise level; it selects a cubic Savitzky-Golay window of 7, 15, 25 or 41 samples (Savitzky & Golay, 1964). The filter gives smoothed states and their derivatives; half a window is trimmed at both ends, where the fit is one-sided.

3.2Support selection

All trajectories are stacked and every library column is scaled to unit RMS. For each equation, STLSQ is run on the normalised columns for 30 thresholds spaced logarithmically between 10^-4 and 0.5 of the largest least-squares coefficient. Each distinct support is scored by leave-one-trajectory-out squared error; the sparsest support within 10% of the best error is kept and refit by least squares on all data, and the coefficients are mapped back to the original scale.

3.3Complexity

For n samples and p library terms each STLSQ run costs O(n p²); with 30 thresholds, d equations and 8 folds per distinct support the total is a few hundred least-squares solves per system, 20 to 75 seconds for 20 systems on two cores.

4Design Rationale

A fixed threshold mixes two scales: the size of a coefficient and the size of the term it multiplies. Normalising the columns makes the threshold a statement about a term's contribution to the derivative. Cross-validation across trajectories penalises spurious terms that only fit noise along one trajectory, and the 10% tolerance prefers parsimony when supports explain the data equally well. On the development sets the reference halves the extrapolation error of STLSQ (0.07 to 0.11 against 0.18 to 0.30) but fails, like the baseline, on most random sparse systems with four variables and on systems whose trajectories excite few terms.

5Scope and Limitations

Pointwise derivatives remain the main source of error at 1% noise; the noise estimate is coarse; the support path is greedy, so terms masked by a correlated term are lost; and nothing exploits the shared structure across equations or the inclusion statistics an ensemble would give.
