k-mer Spectrum with Ridge Regression: An Alignment-Free Baseline for Regulatory Element Classification

ABench reference submissionStandard reference for task statement standard v2.1
Abstract

This submission gives an alignment-free baseline for three Genomic Benchmarks tasks (Grešová et al., 2023). Each sequence is represented by its normalised k-mer spectrum for k = 1 to 6, in the spirit of the spectrum kernel (Leslie et al., 2002), log-scaled and standardised, and a linear classifier is fitted per dataset by ridge regression in closed form, with the regulariser chosen on a seeded validation split. The baseline is position-free by construction; the anchor's long-convolution model (Nguyen et al., 2023) shows how position-aware, single-nucleotide models go further.

1Introduction

Regulatory elements such as promoters, enhancers and open chromatin are recognisable partly by composition (GC content, CpG islands, short motifs) and partly by the arrangement of motifs. A k-mer spectrum captures the first part and is a standard baseline; whatever entries gain over it measures how much positional and long-range structure their models exploit.

Changes relative to the anchor. Each item states what this submission does differently and where it is in the code.

  1. Normalised k-mer spectra for k = 1..6 replace the anchor's single-nucleotide long-convolution encoder. — model.py: spectrum
  2. A closed-form ridge readout to ±1 labels per dataset, with the regulariser chosen on a seeded split, replaces fine-tuning. — train.py: fit_one

2Related Work

The spectrum kernel compares sequences through the counts of their length-k substrings (Leslie et al., 2002). Convolutional networks learn motif detectors directly (Alipanahi et al., 2015), and HyenaDNA uses implicit long convolutions to process sequences at single-nucleotide resolution (Nguyen et al., 2023).

HyenaDNA models long-range order at single-nucleotide resolution after pretraining; k-mer spectra capture composition only; this reference is the latter and leaves positional and long-range structure as the gap.

3Method

3.1Features and classifier

For each k in 1..6, all k-mers containing only A, C, G or T are counted and divided by the number of such k-mers, giving 5,460 frequencies per sequence; values are mapped by log(1 + 1000 f) and standardised. A bias is appended and weights solve the ridge problem to ±1 targets; λ ∈ {10, 100, 1000, 10000} is chosen on a 10% split of the training data drawn with the seed, and the final weights are refitted on all training data.

3.2Complexity

Spectrum extraction is O(L · 6) per sequence of length L. With d = 5,461 features, forming FᵀF costs O(n d²) and solving O(d³); prediction is O(L + d) per sequence.

4Design Rationale

Frequencies rather than counts make sequences of different lengths comparable, and the log map compresses the heavy tail of frequent short k-mers so that informative 5- and 6-mers are not drowned out. Including all k from 1 to 6 lets the linear model combine composition with short motifs.

5Scope and Limitations

The representation discards motif positions, spacing and strand, and longer motifs are only seen through their 6-mer pieces; learned position-aware models address these limits.
