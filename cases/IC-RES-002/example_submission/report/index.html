ABench:IC-RES-002 reference submission

ResNets with Averaging Shortcuts and Zero-Initialised Residual Branches

ABench reference submissionStandard reference for task statement standard v2.1
Abstract

This submission keeps the anchor's ResNet-18 and ResNet-50 (He et al., 2016) at the two budgets and applies two tricks that cost no compute: downsampling shortcuts that average 2 × 2 pixels before a stride-1 projection (ResNet-D) and residual branches whose last batch-normalisation scale starts at zero, so that every block starts as the identity (He et al., 2019; Goyal et al., 2017). The deep stem of ResNet-C was left out because it would exceed the small budget at 128 pixels. It has not been run yet: it needs PyTorch and a GPU, which were not available where the case was built.

1Introduction

With ten classes and fewer than ten thousand training images, a backbone's inductive biases and its behaviour early in training matter as much as its capacity. The case fixes the recipe and two compute budgets, so the backbone is the only variable.

Changes relative to the anchor. Each item states what this submission does differently and where it is in the code.

  1. Downsampling shortcuts average 2 × 2 pixels before a stride-1 projection instead of a strided projection. — model.py: shortcut
  2. The last batch-normalisation scale of every residual branch is initialised to zero. — model.py: Basic, Bottleneck, ResNet.__init__

2Related Work

Residual networks made deep convolutional networks trainable (He et al., 2016); a collection of refinements to the shortcut, stem and training improved them without extra cost (He et al., 2019); zero-initialised residual branches stabilise large-batch training (Goyal et al., 2017). Later designs changed the blocks themselves: inverted residuals (Sandler et al., 2018), compound scaling (Tan & Le, 2019) and the modernised ConvNeXt design (Liu et al., 2022).

3Method

The stages, widths and depths are ResNet-18 (basic blocks, 2-2-2-2) and ResNet-50 (bottlenecks, 3-4-6-3) with the 7 × 7 stem and max pooling. Every shortcut that changes resolution is average pooling with a 2 × 2 window followed by a 1 × 1 convolution and batch normalisation; every residual branch ends in a batch normalisation whose scale is initialised to 0. At 128 pixels the variants need 1.18 and 2.67 GFLOPs.

4Design Rationale

A strided 1 × 1 shortcut discards three quarters of the activations it downsamples; averaging first keeps them at the same cost. Identity-initialised blocks make the early network shallow in effect, which helps when 20 epochs leave little time to recover from a poor start.

5Scope and Limitations

The changes are small by design; neither uses the slack between the variants' FLOPs and the budgets, and the reference has not been run yet.
