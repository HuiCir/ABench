Pre-RMSNorm with Query-Key Normalisation

ABench reference submissionStandard reference for task statement standard v2.1
Abstract

This submission keeps the anchor's pre-normalisation with RMSNorm (Zhang & Sennrich, 2019) and adds one change: queries and keys are RMS-normalised per head after the rotary embedding, with a learned gain per head dimension. QK normalisation bounds the attention logits, whose uncontrolled growth was identified as a source of loss spikes and divergence at high learning rates (al., 2023; al., 2024). It adds four gain vectors per layer and no matrix multiplication. It has not been run yet: it needs PyTorch and a GPU, which were not available where the case was built.

1Introduction

Where a transformer normalises its activations decides how far the learning rate can be pushed before training becomes unstable. Pre-normalisation made deep transformers trainable without warm-up tricks (Xiong et al., 2020), and RMSNorm made it cheaper (Zhang & Sennrich, 2019); at learning rates a few times above the usual one, small models still show loss spikes that trace back to attention.

Changes relative to the anchor. Each item states what this submission does differently and where it is in the code.

  1. Queries and keys are RMS-normalised per head after the rotary embedding, with learned gains initialised to head_dim^(1/4). — norm.py: PreRMSNormQK.make_layer, PreRMSNormQK.qk

2Related Work

Post-normalisation, the original layout (Vaswani et al., 2017), needs careful warm-up; pre-normalisation removes that need (Xiong et al., 2020). Residual scaling such as DeepNorm stabilises very deep post-norm models (Wang et al., 2022); ReZero replaces normalisation by a gated residual initialised at zero (Bachlechner et al., 2021). Normalising queries and keys was introduced for attention (Henry et al., 2020) and became the remedy for attention logit growth in large vision transformers (al., 2023); small-scale proxies reproduce that instability at high learning rates and show QK normalisation removes it (al., 2024).

3Method

Each layer computes x ← x + attn(RMSNorm₁(x)) and x ← x + mlp(RMSNorm₂(x)); inside attention, after the rotary embedding, q ← g_q ⊙ q / rms(q) and k ← g_k ⊙ k / rms(k) per head, with gains of size head_dim initialised to head_dim^(1/4), so that the product of the two gains restores the scale √head_dim that the dot product of two unit-RMS vectors lacks. The final RMSNorm before the tied output head is unchanged.

4Design Rationale

With fixed AdamW and a high peak learning rate, attention logits can grow until the softmax saturates; gradients through a saturated softmax vanish for most positions and spike for a few. Normalising queries and keys caps the logits by the learned gains, which grow only as fast as the optimiser moves them. The change targets the 4e-3 runs; at 1e-3 it should be neutral.

5Scope and Limitations

It does not address output-logit divergence, which Wortsman et al. counter with a z-loss that this interface does not allow; it does not change the residual stream itself; and it has not been run yet.
