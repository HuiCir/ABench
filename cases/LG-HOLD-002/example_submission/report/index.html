Lexical Overlap with a Conditional-Logit Scorer: A Non-Neural Baseline for CaseHOLD

ABench reference submissionStandard reference for task statement standard v2.1
Abstract

This submission gives a non-neural baseline for selecting the holding of a cited case. Each candidate holding is compared with the citing context through tf-idf cosine similarities of unigrams and bigrams (Salton & Buckley, 1988) and a few length and negation cues, and a linear scorer over these features is trained with a softmax across the five candidates, i.e. a conditional logit model (McFadden, 1974). It deliberately ignores the provided roberta-base encoder (Liu et al., 2019), so that the value of pretrained cross-encoders, which the anchor shows to be large on CaseHOLD (Zheng et al., 2021), is measured by the entries.

1Introduction

In CaseHOLD the citing context usually paraphrases or partially quotes the holding it relies on, and the four distractors are holdings of other cases. Word overlap is therefore informative but not sufficient: distractors often share legal vocabulary, and some differ from the answer only by negation or scope.

Changes relative to the anchor. Each item states what this submission does differently and where it is in the code.

  1. Lexical tf-idf cosine similarities and length features replace the anchor's fine-tuned BERT-family cross-encoders. — model.py: features
  2. A linear conditional-logit scorer over the five candidates is fitted by gradient descent. — train.py: main

2Related Work

The anchor fine-tunes BERT-style encoders as multiple-choice cross-encoders and finds that domain pretraining helps most on this hard, domain-specific task (Zheng et al., 2021). Tf-idf weighting with cosine similarity is the classical lexical relevance model (Salton & Buckley, 1988).

Pretrained encoders read the citing context and candidates jointly; lexical overlap scorers are cheap but blind to negation and scope; this reference is the latter and leaves the provided roberta-base unused, which is the gap.

3Method

3.1Features and scorer

Inverse document frequencies are estimated from all training prompts and holdings. For a prompt p and candidate h the features are the cosine similarity of their sublinear tf-idf vectors over unigrams and over bigrams, log(1 + |h|), the fraction of h's word types that occur in p, and an indicator for "not" in h. Each feature is centred across the five candidates of a question. Weights w maximise the conditional likelihood softmax_j(w · f(p, h_j)) of the correct candidate on 20,000 seeded training questions by gradient descent.

3.2Complexity

Feature extraction is linear in the number of tokens of a question; training is O(T · n · 5 · d) for T iterations and d = 6 features. Prediction is linear in the tokens of the question.

4Design Rationale

Centring features within a question removes properties shared by all candidates, so the scorer learns only what distinguishes the correct holding. Bigram similarity rewards partial quotation, which is common in citing contexts. The conditional-logit loss matches the evaluation, where exactly one candidate is chosen.

5Scope and Limitations

Lexical features cannot resolve paraphrase, negation scope or legal reasoning; a fine-tuned cross-encoder over the prompt and each candidate is the natural next step and is what the provided encoder enables.
