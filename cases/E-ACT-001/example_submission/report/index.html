Nearest-Neighbour Action Chunks: A Non-Parametric Imitation Baseline for PushT

ABench reference submissionStandard reference for task statement standard v2.1
Abstract

This submission gives a non-parametric baseline for imitation learning on PushT. The policy standardises the 18-dimensional keypoint observation, retrieves the five nearest demonstration frames and executes the average of their next eight demonstrated actions before querying again. Executing short action sequences follows action chunking (Zhao et al., 2023) and the receding-horizon execution of Diffusion Policy (Chi et al., 2023); retrieval in place of a learned model follows non-parametric imitation (Pari et al., 2022). Averaging neighbours collapses the multimodality of human pushing strategies, which is precisely what generative action heads are designed to keep.

1Introduction

PushT requires precise, contact-rich pushing, and human demonstrations solve it in different ways from similar states. A policy that regresses the mean action blurs these modes; the anchor shows that modelling the action distribution with a diffusion process, and predicting action sequences, both help (Chi et al., 2023).

Changes relative to the anchor. Each item states what this submission does differently and where it is in the code.

  1. Retrieval of the five nearest demonstration frames in standardised keypoint space replaces the anchor's diffusion action head. — policy.py: _Policy.act (nearest neighbours)
  2. The average of the neighbours' next eight actions is executed open loop before the next query. — policy.py: _Policy.act (queue); train.py: main (chunks)

2Related Work

Action chunking predicts k future actions at once and executes them, reducing the effective horizon and the compounding of errors (Zhao et al., 2023). Nearest-neighbour policies act by retrieving demonstrations with similar observations and are strong baselines in low-data imitation (Pari et al., 2022).

Diffusion Policy models multimodal action distributions with a learned generative head; nearest-neighbour retrieval needs no training but averages modes away; that is the gap this baseline leaves open.

3Method

3.1Policy

Training stores the standardised observations o_i = [agent position, 8 keypoints] and, for each frame, the chunk of its next eight actions within the same episode (repeating the last action at the episode end). At run time, if the action queue is empty, the five frames nearest in Euclidean distance are found and the element-wise mean of their chunks is queued; each step pops one action.

3.2Complexity

Training is O(N) to build the chunks for N = 25,650 frames. Each query costs O(N · 18) for the distances; with one query every eight steps this is far below the rollout budget.

4Design Rationale

Keypoint observations make Euclidean distance meaningful after standardisation, so retrieval needs no learned embedding. Committing to eight actions keeps the end-effector moving coherently through a push instead of dithering between neighbours, and averaging five neighbours smooths jitter in the human actions.

5Scope and Limitations

Averaging over neighbours that push in different directions produces an action no demonstrator took; a generative action head (diffusion, flow matching or a mixture) avoids this. The policy also does not generalise beyond states close to the demonstrations.
