The DDPM Recipe on a Four-Hour Budget, Sampled with DDIM

ABench reference submissionStandard reference for task statement standard v2.1
Abstract

This submission reproduces the anchor's CIFAR-10 recipe (Ho et al., 2020) within the session: a 35.7M-parameter UNet with self-attention at 16 × 16 resolution, ε-prediction with the simple mean-squared loss over uniformly drawn timesteps of a linear β schedule, Adam (Kingma & Ba, 2015) with warm-up, dropout 0.1, horizontal flips and an exponential moving average of the weights. Images are generated with deterministic DDIM (Song et al., 2021) on 25 or 100 uniformly spaced steps. It is the calibration point of the case; recipes that converge faster (Nichol & Dhariwal, 2021; Karras et al., 2022) are left to entries.

1Introduction

The quality of a diffusion model trained on a fixed budget depends on how much each training step teaches the network and on how well a few sampling steps use what it learned. The anchor trained for 800,000 steps; the session allows roughly an eighth of that.

Changes relative to the anchor. Each item states what this submission does differently and where it is in the code.

  1. Training stops at a wall-clock limit (about an eighth of the anchor's steps) instead of 800,000 steps. — train.py: main (--max-hours)
  2. The forward pass runs under bf16 autocast. — train.py: main
  3. Sampling uses deterministic DDIM with x₀ clipping on 25 or 100 steps instead of the anchor's 1,000-step ancestral sampler. — model.py: generate

2Related Work

The anchor's ε-prediction with the simple loss and a linear schedule set the standard recipe (Ho et al., 2020). Learned variances and a cosine schedule improved likelihood and sampling speed (Nichol & Dhariwal, 2021); DDIM replaced the stochastic sampler with a deterministic one that tolerates large steps (Song et al., 2021); EDM separated the design choices of preconditioning, noise distribution in training and the ODE solver, reaching much better FID at a given budget (Karras et al., 2022).

Most of these improvements were measured at full training length; which of them matter most when training is eight times shorter is the open question.

3Method

3.1Model, training and sampling

The UNet has four resolution levels with 128, 256, 256 and 256 channels, two residual blocks per level, self-attention at the second level, group normalisation and sinusoidal time embeddings, in the configuration of the published CIFAR-10 model (Ho et al., 2020). Training draws t uniformly from 0 … 999, noises the image with the closed-form forward process and regresses the noise, with Adam at 2e-4 after 5,000 warm-up steps, gradient clipping at 1.0, batch 128 and an EMA with decay 0.9999, until the step or time limit. Sampling runs DDIM with x₀ clipping on S uniformly spaced timesteps.

3.2Complexity

One UNet forward and backward pass per image per step; sampling costs S forward passes per image.

4Design Rationale

Reproducing the anchor's recipe unchanged makes the case's calibration point the anchor itself under the case's budget, so gains are attributable to the entry's changes.

5Scope and Limitations

The linear schedule and uniform timestep sampling spend many steps on nearly noise-free inputs, the EMA decay is tuned for much longer training, and DDIM with 25 steps is far from the best few-step sampler.
