Mixing an Orthogonalised Momentum with a Clipped Adaptive Direction

ABench reference submissionStandard reference for task statement standard v2.1
Abstract

This submission is the outcome of a deliberately small search in the case's primitive language, in the spirit of the symbolic discovery that produced Lion (Chen et al., 2023). Each candidate mixes two update directions computed from the same momentum — sign, row or global normalisation, Newton-Schulz orthogonalisation as in Muon (Jordan et al., 2024), or an Adam-style ratio clipped to [-1, 1] (Kingma & Ba, 2015) — over a grid of momentum constants. Candidates more similar than 0.97 to a known optimizer on the public gradient stream are discarded; among the rest the one whose updates point most along the negative gradient is kept: half orthogonalised momentum, half clipped adaptive direction.

1Introduction

Searching programs instead of hand-designing update rules needs two filters: one that rejects rediscoveries of known optimizers cheaply, and one that measures training quality. The case provides the first as a NumPy screen.

Changes relative to the anchor. Each item states what this submission does differently and where it is in the code.

  1. The update mixes orthogonalised momentum with an Adam-style ratio clipped to [-1, 1], with equal weights, instead of Lion's sign of an interpolated momentum. — program.yaml
  2. Candidates are filtered by similarity to known optimizers on a gradient stream before any training. — search.py: main

2Related Work

Lion was found by evolutionary search over programs and simplified afterwards (Chen et al., 2023). Muon orthogonalises the momentum of matrix parameters (Jordan et al., 2024); Adam divides by a running second-moment estimate (Kingma & Ba, 2015).

3Method

120 candidates: 10 pairs of directions, mixing weight 0.25, 0.5 or 0.75, b1 in {0.9, 0.95}, b2 in {0.98, 0.995}. The screen (seed 17) gives each candidate's largest similarity to the known optimizers and its mean descent cosine; 113 candidates stay at or below 0.97, and the best by descent cosine (0.751, closest known rule at 0.78) is submitted with a learning rate of 1e-3.

4Design Rationale

Orthogonalised momentum equalises the scale of a matrix update across directions; the clipped adaptive term adds per-coordinate scaling that orthogonalisation removes. Mixing them gives a rule that is close to neither parent.

5Scope and Limitations

The search stops at the screen: candidates were not ranked by training runs, and the learning rate was not tuned. The descent cosine on a synthetic stream is a weak proxy for training loss. Training has not been run here.
