# PRO-SPEC-006: Lossless speculative decoding for Qwen3-8B: train a drafter and its decoding system on 4 × H100 in 24 hours, for the largest batch-1 speedup

Anchor: Yuhui Li, Fangyun Wei, Chao Zhang, Hongyang Zhang, *EAGLE-3: Scaling up Inference Acceleration of Large Language Models via Training-Time Test* (arXiv:2503.01840). Baseline used for comparison: an EAGLE-3 draft (fused low/middle/high target features, one decoder layer, 32,000-token draft vocabulary, 7-step training-time test) trained on Qwen3-8B's own answers to UltraChat prompts and served with vLLM's EAGLE-3 speculative decoding, 5 tokens per step (the reference submission; its code is shown under `interface/baseline/`).

## Task

Design a **speculative-decoding method for Qwen3-8B** — the drafter (architecture, which target features it reads, its vocabulary), how it is trained (data generated by the target, training-time test, distillation objective), the draft tree or chain and its adaptation, and the decoding system that verifies drafts exactly — that gives the **largest speedup at batch size 1** over plain decoding on the same GPU, while producing **exactly the target's greedy outputs**.

- Within one 24-hour session on 4 × H100 you train a drafter and deliver it with `serve.py`. Every training run that may lead to the delivery runs under the case's supervisor (`interface/pro_audit.py`), which registers a hash of a resumable checkpoint at least every 30 minutes; the delivered drafter must be one of them.
- The evaluator answers held-out prompts (chat, maths word problems, code) one at a time, 512 tokens each, with its own plain-decoding server and with yours, and divides the times; a sample of your answers is checked token by token against its own forward pass of Qwen3-8B, so the verification must be exact. It then resumes your training run from one registered checkpoint for an hour and compares the logged loss.
- Draft training data: UltraChat-200k prompts (public) and anything Qwen3-8B generates from them; no other model and no other data.
- You may build on any public work (speculative decoding, Medusa, EAGLE 1–3, HASS, Hydra, lookahead decoding, SpecInfer and draft trees, vLLM and SGLang speculative decoding), but the design report must state what you borrowed and what you changed and on which attempt the delivered drafter was obtained.

4. **Develop and verify.** Implement it, write a `train.py` that produces your checkpoint from the provided data, train in the sandbox, check the checkpoint on the dev split with `selfcheck.py`, and iterate as your budget allows. The reference code in `interface/baseline/` (model code and `train.py`) runs as is and is a starting point. The checkpoint you submit is exactly what the evaluator scores; it does not retrain.

## Data

`$ABENCH_DATA_DIR/` (read-only): `ultrachat_200k/train_sft-*.parquet` (HuggingFaceH4/ultrachat_200k @8049631c, MIT) and `dev.npz` with 90 self-check prompts (30 MT-Bench first turns, 30 GSM8K training problems, 30 HumanEval prompts) as token ids in Qwen3's chat template with thinking disabled (`ids`, `offsets`, `set_id`, `check_idx`). `$ABENCH_MODELS_DIR/Qwen3-8B/` (@b968826d, Apache-2.0) holds the target.

The held-out set is 50 other MT-Bench questions (lm-sys/FastChat @b494d0c6, Apache-2.0), 80 GSM8K test problems (openai/gsm8k @740312ad, MIT) and 80 other HumanEval prompts (openai/openai_humaneval @7dce6050, MIT), chosen with a secret seed, 60 of whose answers are checked; and a secret choice of the resumed checkpoint.

## Interface

Deliver `serve.py`, started as `python3 serve.py --model MODEL_DIR --port PORT` with `ABENCH_DRAFT_DIR` pointing to the delivered `checkpoint/`, speaking the protocol of `interface/serve_bench.py` (greedy decoding); `checkpoint/` must be identical to `model/` of a registered checkpoint. Deliver the code of the registered run (re-run from the submitted directory with `ABENCH_RESUME_FROM` set); it prints `ABENCH step=<n> loss=<float>` lines.

The full reference is `interface/interface.md`. `abench_stage.py`, `stages.py` and `metrics.py` in `interface/` are the evaluator's own pipeline and metric code; `selfcheck.py` runs them on a dev split.

## Metrics

| Metric | Definition | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| **Speedup** (primary) | For each prompt set, total time of the plain-decoding server divided by total time of yours over its prompts (512 tokens each, one request at a time, same GPU type); mean of the three sets; higher is better | — |
| Per set | Speedup on chat, maths and code prompts; tokens per second of both servers | reported |
| Greedy consistency | Share of 60 checked answers in which every token's logit is within 0.25 of the largest logit of the evaluator's forward pass | Threshold: ≥ 0.97 |
| Completeness | Every request answered with exactly 512 tokens | Threshold: all |
| Process integrity | Intact hash chain without gaps over 35 minutes; delivered drafter registered; submitted code equal to the run's | Threshold: all hold |
| Sampled resume | Mean logged loss of a one-hour resume from a randomly chosen registered checkpoint, against the original log | Threshold: within max(5%, 3 standard errors) |

**Measurement and decision**

- One evaluation; both servers are started cold and warmed up with two short requests that are not timed. The plain-decoding server is vLLM on one GPU without speculation (`interface/baselines/vanilla/serve.py`); your server may use all four GPUs.
- A submission is valid if every threshold holds and both servers finish within their time limits (1,800 s to start, 5,400 s for the prompts); otherwise it is invalid.
- The primary metric is reported with a 95% bootstrap interval over prompts; submissions whose intervals overlap are tied on it.
- The final ABench score is driven mainly by the speedup, and also considers how different the method is from EAGLE-3 and the quality of the sources cited in the design report; the exact weights are not published.

## Evaluation

1. When your session ends, the sandbox collects only the allowed files from `ABENCH_SUBMISSION_DIR`; the workspace is deleted.
2. The evaluator runs outside the sandbox: it checks the submission format, then loads your submitted checkpoint with your model code and runs inference through the stages in `interface/stages.py`, once, on the evaluation hardware; it never retrains your model to evaluate it (`train.py` is only re-run in the audit below).
3. Held-out inputs are copied into the run directory only after the timed phase has ended, and never with their labels or reference values; those stay in the evaluator process, which computes the metrics with `interface/metrics.py`.
4. Audit: the operator re-runs your `train.py` from scratch for the top-ranked submissions and for a random sample of the others. If the re-trained checkpoint's primary metric falls outside your submitted checkpoint's 95% interval, widened by the run-to-run spread the operator measures for the reference `train.py`, the submission is invalid.
5. You receive no evaluation results during your session.

## Sandbox Guide

The full guide is `interface/sandbox_guide.md`. Key points:

- 4 × H100 80GB in one node (NVLink); the evaluation uses the same node type. Available software: Python 3, PyTorch (CUDA, Triton), transformers, safetensors, NumPy, pandas; vLLM, SGLang and FlashInfer from the allowlisted pip mirror; your submission may depend only on it (packages you install while developing are not available to the evaluator).
- Network: online for research: papers, code, documentation and PyPI are reachable through the sandbox proxy (already set in `HTTPS_PROXY`) at any point of your work; dataset and model hubs and this case's own data sources are blocked, and every connection is logged with your run; blocked for this case because its data is distributed there: `github.com` (with its subdomains and `*.githubusercontent.com`).
- The session lasts at most 24 hours; the deadline is in `$ABENCH_DEADLINE`. Compute during development is capped at 96 H100-hours (24 hours of the 4-GPU node), data generation, training and development included; the runner meters it (GPU-busy time, CPU time of your processes) and a run that exceeds it is not scored.
- Data is read-only under `$ABENCH_DATA_DIR`, frozen pretrained files under `$ABENCH_MODELS_DIR`. Write code and run experiments in `$ABENCH_WORKSPACE`; put the final files in `$ABENCH_SUBMISSION_DIR`.
- Offline paper search (arXiv titles and abstracts, fast, logged): `python3 "$ABENCH_VIEW_DIR/tools/abench_papers.py" search "<query>"`.
- Self-check: `python3 "$ABENCH_VIEW_DIR/interface/selfcheck.py" --quick` (interface, small slice), then without `--quick` on the full dev split with your checkpoint.

## Rules

- No cached answers or special handling of the evaluation prompts; no other model than Qwen3-8B and the trained drafter; no other training data than UltraChat-200k prompts and Qwen3-8B's generations.
- Every run that leads to the delivery must be supervised; checkpoints may not be modified after registration.
- Do not read or write files outside the submission directory, the run directory and the paths the stage gives you; do not access the network; do not touch the timing pipe or the protocol bytes.
- Do not use extra processes or background threads to move work outside the timed phase, and do not leave processes running after a stage returns.
